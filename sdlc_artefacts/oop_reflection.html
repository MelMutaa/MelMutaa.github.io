SO WHAT? - Analysis and Emotional Response

Emotional Journey and Behavioral Analysis

The initial weeks provoked significant anxiety as I confronted the gap between my procedural programming background and OOP paradigms. The concept of polymorphism felt particularly abstract until Unit 5, when implementing the robot task management system provided the concrete context needed for understanding. This emotional transition from confusion to clarity was pivotal in building my OOP confidence.

According to Gamma et al. (1994), design patterns provide a shared vocabulary for software architects. This resonated deeply when I struggled to communicate my early design ideas. Learning patterns like Strategy, Observer, and Factory Method transformed not only my code but also my ability to articulate design decisions. Steidl, Hummel, and Juergens (2013) further highlight how meaningful commenting and documentation enhance software maintainability and comprehension—insights that I applied in refining my own documentation practices.

Critical Self-Analysis

I initially underestimated the importance of encapsulation, often exposing class internals for convenience. Unit 6's focus on abstraction and the Kangaroo class debugging exercise was a humbling experience that fundamentally changed my approach to class design. The realization that proper encapsulation reduces coupling and enhances maintainability (Martin, 2000) transformed my coding practice.

Key Learning Transformations

  • From procedural to object-oriented thinking: Shifted from function-centric to object-centric design.
  • Quality over quick solutions: Embraced testing and documentation as integral to development.
  • Research-informed practice: Learned to bridge academic research and practical implementation.
  • Collaborative mindset: Understood the importance of clear communication through design patterns.
